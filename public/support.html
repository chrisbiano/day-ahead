Reporting a problem from inside the app

Open Settings and use Report a problem. It sends your message along with the app version and any recent errors, which usually means we can find the cause without asking you to reproduce it.

Questions that come up most

Notifications aren't arriving

Open Settings → Notifications and check they're on for the device you're holding — they're enabled per device, not per account. Then use Send a test notification. If the test arrives but reminders don't, the reminder time is the thing to check; if the test doesn't arrive either, your device's own notification settings are blocking Day Ahead.

My calendar or email stopped updating

Google connections expire, usually after a password change or a security review on your Google account. Open Settings, disconnect the account, and connect it again. Nothing you've written is affected — tasks and notes are stored separately from the connection.

Why does Day Ahead need access to Gmail?

To show you what's waiting on a reply and to send the replies you write. It reads the sender, subject, and preview line to sort your inbox; it never sends anything without you pressing send. If you'd rather not connect email at all, the calendar and task side of Day Ahead works on its own.

Can I get my data out?

Yes. Settings → Export my data produces a readable document of everything Day Ahead holds about you — tasks, notes, preferences, and connected accounts — which you can save or print to PDF. No request needed and no waiting.

How do I delete my account?

Settings → Delete my account. You'll see exactly what will be removed before anything happens. Deletion is immediate and permanent — it revokes Day Ahead's access to your Google account and erases your data from our systems, so export first if you want a copy. See the privacy policy for what that covers.

Is my email being read by AI?

The sender, subject, and preview line of incoming mail are sent to a third-party AI service to sort your inbox and write your morning brief. Full message bodies aren't, your mail isn't used to train any model, and nothing is shared with advertisers. The privacy policy names the provider and sets this out in full.
